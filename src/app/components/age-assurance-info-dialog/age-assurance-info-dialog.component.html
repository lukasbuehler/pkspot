Why PK Spot checks adult eligibility

shield

Public identity needs a higher bar

A public profile can expose a picture, city, biography and links to anyone—including search engines. PK Spot keeps that profile private unless an age provider returns an independently checked 18+ result.

Core features remain available

Adult verification is not required for browsing the map, saving or visiting Spots, or ordinary participation. A platform may still require restrictions for a child account or pending parental consent.

Sharing is separate from verification

Google Play first asks whether it may share an age range with PK Spot. A birthday entered on a Google Account is self-declared and does not become independently checked just because it is shared. Google controls when identity, card, selfie or other age-verification choices are offered.

What PK Spot receives

PK Spot asks only for an age range, such as under 13, 13–17 or 18+. PK Spot does not ask Apple for your exact age or birthday. It stores the age band, a broad evidence category and the resulting eligibility—not an exact birth date, identity document, bank account or card number. It also records the provider and method category, when the result was checked, and the policy basis used for the decision. This lets PK Spot withdraw approvals if a later signal changes or a method is found unsuitable. Self-declared age alone does not unlock a public profile.

Browser access

Browser-only accounts can use the core service. For now, public-profile eligibility must be established through a supported mobile platform. Additional privacy-preserving verification choices may be added later.
